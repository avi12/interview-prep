## Any questions for us?

This is not really a question. The interviewer expects you to ask something.

- Having none can make you look less interested (Tech Interview Handbook)
- Your questions show what you care about
- You also learn about the job, including red flags

Ask **different things** to different people:

| Who | Ask about |
|---|---|
| Recruiter | The process, the format, next steps |
| Hiring manager | Day to day, the team's focus, how they work |
| Engineer | Current challenges, how the team works together, growth |

> **Why:** an interview goes both ways. You are choosing a team too, and you will spend most of your week with it.

```narrate
At the end they ask, any questions for us? It is not really a question. The interviewer expects you to ask something, and having none can make you look less interested. Your questions also show what you care about, and they help you spot red flags. Ask different things to different people. Ask the recruiter about the process. Ask the hiring manager about the team. Ask engineers about how they work together.
```

## Five questions to keep ready

Pick three. You will not have time for more.

- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "Where could someone early in their career add the most value?"
- "How do you onboard engineers who are new to the team?"
- "What is the most important problem you would want me to solve?"
- "What do you enjoy most about working here, and what would you change?"

> **Not this:** questions a quick search answers, or questions about pay and promotion. Formation says to save money talk for the negotiation phase.

```narrate
Keep five questions ready, and pick three. What does a typical week look like in this role? Where could someone early in their career add the most value? How do you onboard engineers who are new to the team? What is the most important problem you would want me to solve? And what do you enjoy most about working here? Do not ask things a quick search answers, and save pay questions for later.
```

## Salary: the rules

The recruiter often asks early: "What are your salary expectations?"

1. **Research first.** AllJobs, Drushim, Glassdoor (Nisha lists them)
2. **Ask for their range first.** Early, you know too little
3. **If pressed, give a range,** tied to your level. Say you are flexible about the full package
4. **Do not share your current pay.** You may politely decline
5. **No personal reasons** like a wedding or a mortgage

> **Why:** people hear ranges selectively. A LinkedIn recruiting article says managers hear the bottom and candidates hear the top. So make the bottom of your range a number you would accept.

**Sources differ:** Capital One says put your lowest acceptable number at the bottom. Drushim says aim for the top and never reveal your minimum.

```quiz
q: A recruiter asks your salary expectation in the first call. You know almost nothing about the role. Best answer?
options:
  - One high number, to leave room
  - "Whatever you think is fair"
  - Ask for the range and the scope first. If pressed, give a researched range
answer: 2
why: Capital One advises asking about the budget first, and Nisha says to stay open early and ask about the role. "Whatever you think is fair" is a listed mistake.
```

```narrate
The recruiter often asks, what are your salary expectations? Research first. Then ask for their range, because early on you know too little. If pressed, give a range tied to your level, and say you are flexible about the full package. You may politely decline to share your current pay. Sources disagree on whether the bottom of your range should be your minimum, so make sure even the bottom is a number you would accept.
```

## Salary: what to say

| Moment | English | עברית |
|---|---|---|
| Ask first | "Could you share the salary range for this role?" | "אפשר לדעת מה טווח השכר שהוגדר לתפקיד?" |
| Not yet | "I'd like to understand the role better before I name a number." | "אשמח להבין את התפקיד לעומק לפני שאציין מספר." |
| Pressed | "From my research, similar roles pay [low] to [high]. I'm flexible, depending on the full package." | "לפי בדיקת השוק שלי, תפקידים דומים משלמים בין [מינימום] ל[מקסימום], ואני גמיש/ה בהתאם לחבילה המלאה." |
| Current pay | "I'd rather not share my current pay. I'm focused on the market range for this role." | "אני מעדיף/ה לא לחשוף את השכר הנוכחי שלי. אני מתמקד/ת בטווח השוק של התפקיד." |

> **Trap:** a number with no reason, or a flat "what are you offering?". Nisha lists both as common mistakes.

```narrate
Here are four sentences to learn by heart, in English and Hebrew. First, ask: could you share the salary range for this role? If it is too early: I would like to understand the role better before I name a number. If pressed: from my research, similar roles pay between low and high, and I am flexible depending on the full package. About current pay: I would rather not share it, I am focused on the market range for this role.
```

## An offer arrives

- **Say thanks. Ask for time.** Do not answer in the first minute
- There is usually room to negotiate, and recruiters expect you to (Tech Interview Handbook)
- Below your range? Stay calm and ask what the full package includes (Nisha)

As a junior, you can also ask for things that are not money: training, your first project, your team, a mentor (Tech Interview Handbook).

> **Not this:** accepting on the spot, or saying "the minimum I'd accept is...". Drushim lists both as mistakes.

```narrate
When an offer arrives, say thanks and ask for time. Do not answer in the first minute. The Tech Interview Handbook says there is usually room to negotiate, and recruiters expect it. If it is below your range, stay calm and ask what the full package includes. As a junior you can also ask for training, your first project, your team, or a mentor. Do not accept on the spot.
```

## A rejection arrives

A no is **data**, not a verdict.

- interviewing.io, over 1,000 interviews: only about **20%** of people performed consistently from one interview to the next
- interviewing.io's own data says **43%** of candidates consistently underrate how they did

These come from practice interviews on one platform, so read them as a hint. One result is noisy. Do three things:

1. Email thanks and ask **one specific** question: "Which part of the coding round should I improve?"
2. Write it in the mistake log (lesson 02)
3. Retry. Treat it as a numbers game

Many companies will not answer. Ask once.

```quiz
q: You were rejected after a bad coding round. What does the data suggest?
options:
  - Your ability is fixed and low
  - One result is noisy, so collect feedback, log it, and retry
  - Never apply to that company again
answer: 1
why: interviewing.io found only about 20% of people perform consistently across interviews, so a single result says little.
```

```narrate
A rejection is data, not a verdict. interviewing.io looked at over one thousand interviews and found that only about twenty percent of people performed consistently from one interview to the next. The same company reports that forty-three percent of its candidates underrate how they did. So one result is noisy. Email a thank-you and ask one specific question. Write the answer in your mistake log. Then retry. Many companies will not reply, so ask once.
```

## Say it out loud

Role-play with a friend, or alone with a recorder:

1. The recruiter asks: "What are your salary expectations?" Answer in 3 sentences. Do it in Hebrew and in English
2. The interviewer asks: "Any questions for us?" Ask your three
3. Write the thank-you note in 4 sentences, with one specific detail

Listen back. Did you ask for their range first? Did you avoid a number with no reason?

```narrate
Role-play it out loud. First, the recruiter asks about your salary expectations. Answer in three sentences, once in Hebrew and once in English. Second, the interviewer asks, any questions for us? Ask your three. Third, write a four sentence thank-you note with one specific detail. Then listen back. Did you ask for their range first? Did you avoid a number with no reason?
```
